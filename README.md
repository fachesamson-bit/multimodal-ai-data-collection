# What Is Egocentric Data?

Egocentric data is recorded from the perspective of the person performing an activity.

Instead of observing a person from a fixed external camera, an egocentric recording captures what the participant sees while performing the task.

Examples include:

* Preparing food
* Washing dishes
* Folding clothes
* Sweeping
* Mopping
* Organizing objects
* Using a smartphone
* Working on a computer
* Performing household activities
* Manipulating everyday objects

This type of data can help AI systems learn about human actions, object interactions, environments, and real-world workflows.

# Data Collection Workflow

A reliable collection process can be summarized as:

**Understand → Prepare → Record → Review → Validate → Submit**

## 1. Understand

Review the activity requirements and determine exactly what needs to be captured.

Important considerations include:

* Required activity
* Required viewpoint
* Recording duration
* Camera orientation
* Environment requirements
* Required objects
* Prohibited content
* Submission requirements

## 2. Prepare

Prepare the recording environment before beginning the activity.

This includes:

* Positioning the camera securely
* Checking framing
* Ensuring adequate lighting
* Removing unnecessary distractions
* Preparing required objects
* Confirming sufficient recording storage

## 3. Record

Perform the activity naturally while maintaining a consistent first-person viewpoint.

The goal is to capture the activity rather than stage unnatural movements solely for the camera.

## 4. Review

Inspect the captured footage for obvious quality problems.

Examples include:

* Excessive camera movement
* Poor framing
* Obstructed view
* Excessive darkness
* Unintended interruptions
* Missing portions of the activity

## 5. Validate

Confirm that the recording satisfies the applicable task requirements.

## 6. Submit

Submit the validated recording through the designated data-collection platform.

# Recording Quality Dimensions

## Viewpoint

The camera should provide a clear first-person perspective appropriate to the activity.

## Framing

Important objects and actions should remain sufficiently visible.

## Lighting

The environment should provide enough illumination to make the activity and objects distinguishable.

## Stability

Camera movement should remain within acceptable limits for the task.

## Completeness

The recording should capture the required activity without important portions being unintentionally omitted.

## Natural Interaction

The participant should perform the activity naturally rather than introducing unnecessary movements.

---

# Common Data-Collection Problems

### Poor Framing

Important actions or objects move outside the camera's useful field of view.

### Excessive Motion

Rapid head or camera movement makes the activity difficult to interpret.

### Insufficient Lighting

Objects and actions become difficult to distinguish.

### Occlusion

The participant's hands or other objects block important interactions.

### Incomplete Activity

The recording does not capture the required sequence from beginning to end.

### Environmental Distractions

Unnecessary people, objects, screens, or background activity interfere with the intended recording.

### Inconsistent Perspective

The camera position changes significantly during the activity, reducing consistency.

# Example Activity

## Activity: Preparing a Simple Beverage

A participant prepares a beverage while recording from a first-person perspective.

### Expected Sequence

1. Gather the required items.
2. Place the container on the work surface.
3. Add the required ingredients.
4. Pour the beverage into the container.
5. Mix or stir the contents.
6. Place the finished beverage on the surface.
7. Complete the activity naturally.

### Data-Quality Considerations

The evaluator should verify that:

* The relevant objects are visible.
* The participant's interactions can be understood.
* The recording remains in a first-person perspective.
* Lighting is sufficient.
* Important actions are not unnecessarily obscured.
* The activity is captured continuously enough to establish the sequence.

# Object Interaction

Egocentric datasets are particularly valuable when they capture interactions between people and objects.

Examples include:

| Interaction | Example                          |
| ----------- | -------------------------------- |
| Picking up  | Lifting a cup from a table       |
| Placing     | Putting an object onto a surface |
| Opening     | Opening a container              |
| Closing     | Closing a drawer                 |
| Pouring     | Pouring liquid into a cup        |
| Folding     | Folding clothing                 |
| Cleaning    | Wiping a surface                 |
| Operating   | Using a keyboard or smartphone   |

These interactions can provide useful information about object identity, hand-object relationships, movement, and activity sequences.

# Data Quality Checklist

Before submitting a recording:

* [ ] Correct activity was performed.
* [ ] First-person perspective is maintained.
* [ ] Camera is securely positioned.
* [ ] Important actions remain visible.
* [ ] Required objects are present.
* [ ] Lighting is adequate.
* [ ] Footage is sufficiently stable.
* [ ] Important interactions are not unnecessarily obstructed.
* [ ] Recording captures the required activity.
* [ ] No unintended interruptions affect the recording.
* [ ] File meets the applicable recording requirements.
* [ ] Final footage has been reviewed before submission.

# Quality-Control Mindset

High-quality multimodal data collection is not simply about recording a video.

The resulting data must be:

**Usable** — important content can be interpreted.

**Consistent** — recordings follow the required viewpoint and methodology.

**Complete** — the intended activity is adequately captured.

**Natural** — human actions represent realistic behavior.

**Clear** — objects and interactions can be distinguished.

**Compliant** — the recording satisfies the applicable task requirements.
